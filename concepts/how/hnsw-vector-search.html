How does a vector database find nearest neighbours fast (HNSW)?

In one lineInstead of comparing the query with every vector, it walks a layered graph of neighbour links: long jumps on sparse upper layers, then finer steps on denser lower layers.

How to read this diagram

Step by step
  1. When a vector is inserted, it's linked to its nearest neighbours; a few vectors are also placed on higher, sparser layers.
  2. A search starts at an entry point on the top layer and keeps moving to whichever neighbour is closer to the query.
  3. When no neighbour is closer, it drops to the next layer and continues from that point.
  4. On the bottom layer, it explores a list of candidates (size ef) and returns the best k.
Key details
  • It's approximate: very fast, with recall usually 95–99%+, tunable through the M and ef parameters.
  • Exact brute-force search is fine up to tens of thousands of vectors.
  • Other index types: IVF (cluster first, then search nearby clusters), often combined with product quantization to compress vectors.
