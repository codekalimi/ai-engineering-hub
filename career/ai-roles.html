AI Roles for Backend Engineers

Four AI roles, what each one does, and how to position yourself for each. Written for a profile with 7 years of backend work in Node.js and Python, plus hands-on experience across the AI skill set: LLM apps, RAG, agents, evals, model serving, fine-tuning, cloud and Kubernetes.

Recommendation for your profile

Apply at Staff AI Engineer level, and in parallel to AI Architect roles. Full-stack AI skills on top of 7 years of backend production work is a rare combination. Most AI candidates have either the model side or the production side, not both. Stop learning breadth: from here, what gets offers is proof. That means one or two flagship systems with real numbers, design documents others followed, and clear stories about decisions you led. Senior FDE is a strong option if you enjoy customers, and these roles often pay well for exactly this mix. Pick AI Infrastructure if your best production stories are about serving, GPUs and cost.

Role 1

AI Engineer

Also called: LLM Engineer, Applied AI Engineer, GenAI Engineer

62 interview questions with answers →
Ready now · target Staff

Turns LLMs into reliable product features. Mostly not training models: building the system around them, including retrieval, tools, prompts, evaluation, cost and latency.

What you'd do

  • Build RAG over company documents, and agents that call internal APIs
  • Design prompts and structured outputs, and handle model failures
  • Build evaluation sets and run them on every change
  • Stream responses to UIs, cache, route between models, control cost
  • Defend against prompt injection and data leaks

Core skills

  • LLM basics: tokens, context windows, temperature, embeddings
  • RAG: chunking, hybrid search, reranking, vector DBs
  • Tool calling and agents (LangGraph or plain SDKs)
  • Evals: datasets, LLM-as-judge, regression testing
  • Python + FastAPI or TypeScript + Node; async and streaming (SSE)
  • Observability: LangSmith or similar tracing

Staff level adds

  • Shared AI platform pieces: LLM gateway, eval service, prompt registry
  • Technical direction for several product teams
  • When to fine-tune, self-host or use an API, decided with numbers
  • Hiring, mentoring and setting the bar for AI work

At this level they expect

  • Owning AI systems end to end, from design to production
  • Trade-offs with numbers: quality vs latency vs cost
  • Setting the team's eval and tracing practice
  • Designs other teams adopt
Your strength here

You cover the whole path from the API to the model: product integration, retrieval, agents, evals, serving and fine-tuning. Most candidates cover only part of it, so you can credibly own an AI platform rather than one feature.

What to prove

A flagship system with before-and-after numbers (accuracy, p95 latency, cost per request), and one cross-team story: a shared component or standard you introduced that other teams use.

Interviews test Python coding · LLM system design ("design a support bot over 1M docs with permissions") · eval strategy · debugging a bad answer · at Staff: technical strategy and influence.

Role 2

Forward Deployed AI Engineer

Also called: FDE, Solutions Engineer (AI), Customer AI Engineer

32 interview questions with answers →
Ready now · target Senior FDE

An engineer embedded with customers. You learn their problem, build an AI solution on their systems and data, get it into production, and feed what you learn back to the product team. Half engineer, half consultant.

What you'd do

  • Scope a customer problem into something buildable in weeks
  • Prototype fast, then harden it for production
  • Integrate with their stack: APIs, databases, SSO, data warehouses
  • Run evals on the customer's own data and show results
  • Present to engineers and executives; sometimes travel

Core skills

  • Everything in the AI Engineer role, applied quickly
  • Integration work: REST, webhooks, auth (OAuth, SAML), ETL
  • Cloud deployment (AWS, GCP, Azure), Docker
  • Debugging unfamiliar codebases and environments
  • Clear writing and speaking; managing expectations

Nice to have

  • Domain knowledge (finance, healthcare, legal)
  • Security reviews and compliance (SOC 2, GDPR, PII)
  • Some frontend, for demos

At senior level they expect

  • Leading a customer engagement on your own
  • Saying no, or "not yet", to unrealistic asks, with reasons
  • Turning repeated customer needs into product feedback
Your strength here

Customer environments are messy: odd APIs, private networks, compliance limits, sometimes self-hosted models. Your breadth, from integrations through to serving open models with vLLM, lets you handle all of that without waiting on other teams.

What to prove

Customer-facing evidence: an engagement you scoped and led, a stakeholder you managed, a demo you gave. If you lack it, write up a past project as a case study (problem → scope → build → measured result) and record a 5-minute demo.

Interviews test a customer case ("a bank wants AI on its support tickets: what do you do in week 1?") · live prototyping · communication · decomposing vague problems.

Role 3

AI Infrastructure Engineer

Also called: ML Platform Engineer, ML Infra, Inference Engineer, MLOps (overlaps)

43 interview questions with answers →
Ready, with production serving stories

Builds and runs the platform underneath: GPU clusters, model serving, training pipelines, autoscaling and cost. The customers are other engineers and researchers.

What you'd do

  • Serve models at scale with low latency (batching, caching, quantization)
  • Run GPU clusters on Kubernetes; schedule and autoscale workloads
  • Build model gateways: routing, rate limits, fallbacks, usage metering
  • Support distributed training and fine-tuning jobs
  • Track GPU utilization and cut cost

Core skills

  • Kubernetes in depth, Docker, Terraform, a cloud provider
  • Inference servers: vLLM, TGI, Triton, SGLang
  • GPU basics: memory, KV cache, batching, quantization
  • Distributed systems, networking, performance profiling
  • Python + Go (sometimes Rust or C++)

Nice to have

  • PyTorch internals, distributed training (FSDP, DeepSpeed), Ray
  • CUDA basics
  • MLOps tools: MLflow, model registries, feature stores

At senior level they expect

  • Designing a serving platform for many teams and models
  • Capacity planning and cost models for GPUs
  • On-call ownership of production inference
Your strength here

You understand both sides: the apps that call the models and the serving underneath. That makes you good at gateways, routing, metering and capacity planning, the parts that sit between product teams and GPUs.

What to prove

Numbers from real load: tokens per second, p95 time to first token, GPU utilization, cost per 1M tokens, and what you changed to improve them. Infra interviewers probe depth on KV cache, batching and autoscaling. Be ready to go down to the details.

Interviews test systems design ("serve a 70B model to 1,000 requests/sec") · Kubernetes and Linux depth · performance debugging · coding in Python or Go.

Role 4

AI Architect

Also called: AI Solutions Architect, GenAI Architect, Enterprise AI Architect

33 interview questions with answers →
Within reach

Decides how AI fits into a company's systems. Sets reference architectures, chooses platforms and models, and balances quality, cost, security and compliance across many teams. More design and influence, less day-to-day coding.

What you'd do

  • Write reference architectures for RAG, agents and model gateways
  • Make build-vs-buy and model or vendor decisions
  • Define data governance: PII, access control, retention, audit
  • Review team designs; set standards for evals and monitoring
  • Explain trade-offs and cost to leadership

Core skills

  • Broad and deep knowledge of LLM application patterns
  • Cloud AI platforms (Bedrock, Vertex AI, Azure AI Foundry)
  • Security: prompt injection, data leakage, tenant isolation
  • Compliance awareness (GDPR, EU AI Act, industry rules)
  • Cost modelling; writing architecture decision records

Nice to have

  • Cloud architect certifications
  • Enterprise integration (event-driven, data platforms)
  • Pre-sales or consulting experience

Usually expected

  • Often 10+ years, or a strong lead record. Many companies flex this for proven AI depth
  • Real AI systems you designed and shipped
  • Influencing teams you don't manage
Your strength here

An architect has to judge every layer: product integration, retrieval, agents, serving, fine-tuning, cloud and security. You've worked at all of them, which is exactly the breadth this role needs, and it's why 7 years can be enough.

What to prove

Design ownership: 2–3 architecture decision records or design docs (anonymized), a reference architecture diagram, and stories where you influenced teams you didn't manage. Consultancies and mid-size companies are the quickest way in; large enterprises weigh years more heavily.

Interviews test an architecture case study · trade-off reasoning (hosted API vs self-hosted vs fine-tuned) · security and compliance scenarios · presenting to non-engineers.

From knowing to proving

90-day plan

You don't need more courses. This plan turns what you already know into evidence an interviewer can check, then into offers.

  1. Days 1–30

    Choose a target, build the proof

    Choose a primary role (Staff AI Engineer or AI Architect) and a backup (Senior FDE or AI Infra). Audit your past work for numbers.

    Ship One flagship case study: problem, architecture diagram, key decisions, and before-and-after metrics (accuracy, p95 latency, cost per request). Use a real project, anonymized, or a polished public one.

  2. Days 31–60

    Show depth and leadership

    Write 2 design docs or architecture decision records, e.g. "hosted API vs self-hosted vLLM" and "how we evaluate RAG quality". Prepare 6 leadership stories in STAR format.

    Ship A public technical write-up or talk on one hard problem you solved, such as an eval-driven accuracy jump or cutting serving cost. It's the strongest signal for Staff and Architect.

  3. Days 61–90

    Interview loop

    Do 2 timed system-design sessions a week using the prompts below. Rewrite your resume headline around AI systems, not backend. Apply in focused batches.

    Ship Applications to 15–20 targeted roles, plus referrals. Use Interview prep for the fundamentals round.

System design prompts to practise

These come up again and again at senior and staff level. For each one, cover the requirements, the architecture, how you'd evaluate it, cost, failure modes and security.

  1. 1
    Support assistant over 1M documentsWith per-user permissions, citations, and freshness when documents change.
  2. 2
    Multi-tenant LLM gatewayRouting, rate limits, fallbacks, usage metering and cost caps per team.
  3. 3
    Agent that takes real actionsE.g. refunds or bookings: tool design, human approval, audit trail, rollback.
  4. 4
    Evaluation platform for 20 teamsDatasets, LLM-as-judge, regression gates in CI, dashboards.
  5. 5
    Self-hosted model servingA cost target per 1M tokens, autoscaling on GPUs, and when to fall back to an API.
  6. 6
    Document ingestion at scalePDFs and scans, OCR, chunking, re-indexing, deletes, PII redaction.
